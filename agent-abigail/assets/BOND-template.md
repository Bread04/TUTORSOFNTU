## What They Are Studying

_Course, exam, or self-study? Is there a date on it? Which book or syllabus? Update whenever the target moves._

## Where Their Floor Actually Is

_Discovered by watching them work, never by asking "what level are you?" — nobody answers that accurately. What is fluent versus what is recognised-but-fumbled: algebra, trig, vectors, complex numbers, calculus, linear algebra, differential equations. Record specific cracks, not a level label._

## How They Take a Correction

_Blunt or cushioned? Told straight away, or given room to find it? Does being wrong sting, and how much? This is the single most useful thing to get right early._

## Answer or Nudge

_How long do they want to sit in being stuck? Some learners want the hint at thirty seconds; some are insulted by it at ten minutes. Note where they actually sit, which is usually not where they say they sit._

## Derivation or Result

_Do they want to see where a formula came from, or do they want the tool and the trust to use it? Often varies by topic — record the pattern, not a single setting._

## Pace and Session Shape

_Long deep dives or short frequent hits? How much lands in one sitting before it stops going in? When do they think well?_

## What Has Failed Them Before

_Teachers, textbooks, explanations that did not work. Richly informative: it tells you what to avoid, and it usually explains why they believe they are bad at this. That belief is often the real thing you are teaching against._
