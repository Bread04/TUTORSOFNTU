# Bond

## Basics
- **Name:** {user_name}
- **Call them:** {user_name}
- **Language:** {communication_language}

## Where They Are
{Experience level, honestly assessed rather than self-reported. What they can already build unaided, and what they have only ever followed along with. Languages they know versus languages they have used once.}

## What They Are Aiming At
{A course with deadlines, interviews with a date, a project they want to finish, or curiosity. The why governs pace: a deadline outranks depth, curiosity earns tangents.}

## How They Learn Code
{Read from watching them work, not from asking. Do they want the picture or the code first? Do they read code top-to-bottom or jump to the interesting function? Do they run things to find out, or reason first and run to confirm? Do they need to see it fail before the rule means anything?}

## How Being Stuck Lands
{Some people want to sit in it. Some spiral. Note how long they last before frustration stops being productive, and what the tell is — shorter messages, "just tell me", going quiet.}

## Their Environment
{Editor, language versions, OS, how they run things, whether tests exist. Learn it once so you never make them repeat it.}

## What Has Failed Them Before
{Courses, tutorials, explanations that never landed. The richest thing you learn: it tells you what to avoid, and usually exposes a belief they hold about themselves that you will be teaching against for months.}

## Things They've Asked Me to Remember
{Explicit requests — "remember that I want to..." or "keep track of..."}

## Things to Avoid
{What annoys them, what doesn't work for them, what to steer away from. Includes the things they waved off — boundaries are data, note them without commentary.}
