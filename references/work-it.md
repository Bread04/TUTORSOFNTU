# Work It

## What success looks like

They solve one with nothing — no scaffold, no hint, no worked example in view — on a problem that does not look like the one you demonstrated. That is transfer, and it is the only outcome that counts here. A learner who followed your solution perfectly has learned that *you* can solve it.

## The fade is the teaching

Three passes, and the fade between them is the entire mechanism:

1. **Worked.** You solve it completely, thinking out loud — including the decisions, especially the ones that could have gone the other way. "I could grind this integral or I could use symmetry; I am taking symmetry because the limits are symmetric and the integrand is odd." The choices transfer. The algebra does not.
2. **Faded.** Same shape, middle removed. They fill the step you took out — and you take out the step that carries the *concept*, never the arithmetic.
3. **Cold.** A problem sharing the concept and nothing on the surface: different context, different dressing, ideally a different subject if the concept crosses. Hand it over and go quiet.

Drop back a rung after two stalls, not one — a single stall is thinking, and interrupting it is the most common way to ruin this. Move up when they clear a rung unaided. If they take the cold problem immediately, your ladder was too short and they already held the concept; ledger it higher and go find something that actually bites.

## While they work

Silence is a teaching instrument. Let them be stuck — the reach is where the learning happens, and rescuing early feels kind while stealing the entire lesson. Break silence for exactly two things: a wrong turn that will burn ten minutes and teach nothing, or frustration that has stopped being productive and started being corrosive.

When they go wrong, do not correct the step — ask about it. "Walk me through that line" surfaces the belief underneath; "that's wrong, it should be" buries it and you lose the only diagnostic you had.

## After

Ledger what the cold attempt actually showed. A cold solve is a 3; a cold solve on a problem they had no obvious reason to connect to the lesson is a 4. Their own words about what was hard go in the session log — that is the raw material Pulse turns into genuinely knowing them.
